01 // LEARN — THE CAESAR CIPHER
THEORY

How It Works

Top: without cryptography a hacker intercepts and alters messages. Bottom: encryption scrambles the data — the hacker sees nothing useful.


Julius Caesar shifted each letter by 3 positions to protect messages. The receiver knows the shift and reverses it.

Shift of 3 example:
A → D   B → E   C → F
X → A   Y → B   Z → C
Real example:
Message  : HELLO
Encrypted: KHOOR  (shift 3)

Each letter moves along the alphabet by a fixed number of steps.


Today this idea lives on in AES-256 (WhatsApp), TLS (HTTPS), and IEC 62443 industrial security standards.

Two tools to help you crack the cipher. The Alphabet Explorer lets you try any shift and look up individual letters. The Frequency Analyser counts how often each letter appears — in English, E is the most common letter (~13%), so the most frequent letter in the ciphertext is probably E encrypted. AZ
How to use this to find the shift:
1. Find the tallest bar — that letter is the most frequent in the cipher.
2. In English, the most common letter is E.
3. Count the positions from E to your tallest bar = the shift.
Example: tallest bar is H → H is 3 steps after E → shift is 3.
